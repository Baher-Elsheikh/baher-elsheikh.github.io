“Impractical” in the NBIC: A Technical Decision, Not a Shortcut

Several NBIC paragraphs allow an alternative method of repair when the normal method is “impractical” or “not practicable”. That flexibility is useful, but it is not a loophole: the decision must rest on technical grounds, and saving time or cost must not be what drives it.

By Baher Elsheikh. November 2026. 4 min read.

Where the NBIC allows an alternative

In several places, the National Board Inspection Code (NBIC) recognises that the method normally required for a repair or inspection cannot always be used, and allows an alternative. Two examples show how the wording works.

NBIC 2.5.3 Alternative welding methods without postweld heat treatment, (a)

“Under certain conditions, postweld heat treatment, in accordance with the original code of construction, may be inadvisable or impractical. In such instances, the following alternative methods may be used.”

NBIC 3.3.4.9 Tube plugging in firetube boilers

“When the replacement of a tube in a firetube boiler is not practicable at the time the defective tube is detected, with the concurrence of the owner, Inspector, and where required, the Jurisdiction, the tube may be plugged using the following course of repair.”

In both cases the preferred method comes first (PWHT to the original code of construction; replacing the tube), and the alternative applies only when the preferred method is shown to be impractical. The tube plugging paragraph also names who must agree: the owner, the Inspector and, where required, the Jurisdiction.

Example: repairs without PWHT

Avoiding postweld heat treatment is one of the most common uses of this flexibility. In a repair, the equipment is already built and often in place. Its geometry, attached components, access, and the risk of distortion or of high and non-uniform residual stresses from local heating can make PWHT difficult, or even harmful to the equipment. These are technical reasons, and they are what the alternative welding methods of 2.5.3 are for.

What these reasons have in common is that they concern the equipment and the quality of the repair. A shorter outage or a lower repair cost is a real benefit, but it says nothing about whether PWHT is technically inadvisable.

NB Interpretation 17-01

The National Board has answered this question directly.

NB Interpretation 17-01 (2017 edition). Subject: application of the term “practicable”

Question: May the desire to save time and/or expense be used solely in determining if a repair and/or alteration activity is practicable?

Reply: No. The determination of “practicable” shall be based on technical consideration of the nature and scope of repair and/or alteration activities.

Time and cost can be part of planning a repair, but they cannot be the basis for declaring the normal method impractical. The decision has to be made, and defended, on the nature and scope of the repair.

What a sound justification looks like

  • State the technical reason the normal method is inadvisable or impractical for this repair: what would go wrong, or what cannot be achieved.
  • Show that the alternative is acceptable for the material, thickness and service, within the conditions the NBIC paragraph sets.
  • Check the service, not only the code. Where PWHT is needed for the service environment rather than for the code of construction, avoiding it may not be acceptable even if the code allows an alternative.
  • Get the required concurrence (owner, Inspector and, where required, the Jurisdiction) before the work, not after.
  • Record the basis in the repair documentation, so that the next engineer can see why the alternative was used.

In short

  • “Impractical” means flexibility, not a loophole.
  • The decision is based on technical justification, not convenience.
  • Saving time or cost is not the main driver (NB Interpretation 17-01).
  • Integrity and safety of the equipment come first.

References

  1. National Board Inspection Code (NBIC), ANSI/NB-23, The National Board of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Inspectors: 2.5.3 and 3.3.4.9.
  2. NB Interpretation 17-01, Application of the term “practicable”, 2017 edition.
