Copper Contamination Cracking of Austenitic Stainless Steels

Copper improves stainless steel when it is in the alloy, and cracks it when it is on the surface. How copper rubbed onto 304L or 316L causes intergranular cracking during welding, and how to prevent it.

By Baher Elsheikh. 3 min read.

Copper (Cu) contamination can cause copper contamination cracking (CCC) in austenitic stainless steel weldments when the surface at or near the heat-affected zone (HAZ) is contaminated with copper. The cracks result from liquid metal embrittlement (LME) and are intergranular.

Copper as an alloying element

As an alloying element, copper improves the corrosion resistance of stainless steels in reducing acids such as sulfuric and phosphoric acid. In intermediate (25 to 70%) and concentrated (70 to 99+%) sulfuric acid, the super-austenitic grade 904L, which contains about 1.5% Cu, performs much better than copper-free grades. The benefit of copper in sulfuric acid has long been known.

How copper contamination cracking happens

As a surface contaminant, however, copper is harmful. CCC can occur when copper on the surface is heated above its melting point (about 1083 °C), either in the HAZ during welding or during annealing heat treatment. For comparison, 304L stainless steel melts over a range of about 1400 to 1450 °C. The molten copper spreads very rapidly along the grain boundaries of the HAZ as a liquid film. As the weld cools and shrinks, it imposes a tensile stress that the copper-rich liquid film cannot withstand, and cracks form along these grain boundaries in the HAZ.

The usual scenario is that copper is rubbed onto the stainless steel surface, for example by sliding the stainless steel against a copper chill bar on a seam welder. Microscopic particles of copper rub off and adhere to the stainless steel surface.

Prevention

  • Copper backing bars are often used when welding sheet material. Avoid rubbing the stainless steel against the copper, which transfers copper to the surface.
  • Direct contact, such as with a chill bar, backing bar or alignment clamp, can be acceptable as long as rubbing is avoided.
  • Do not store stainless steel in contact with, or in the same area as, galvanized steel or copper. Surface contamination by copper, zinc or other low-melting metals can embrittle stainless steels.

In short, the surfaces of austenitic stainless steels such as 304L and 316L must be protected against copper being rubbed onto them.

Inspection and identification

  • Penetrant testing (PT) can detect surface cracks initiated by CCC in the HAZ.
  • Metallography can detect the intergranular cracks in the HAZ, and the presence of copper at the grain boundaries in the as-polished condition.
