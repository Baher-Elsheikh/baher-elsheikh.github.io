Weld Buildup on Thinned Pressure Components: Weld the Perimeter First

When a pressure component has thinned from the inside, an external weld buildup can restore the wall. ASME PCC-2 gives a simple rule for the order of the passes: weld the outer perimeter of the buildup area first, then fill the inside. The reasons are practical and metallurgical.

By Baher Elsheikh. November 2026. 3 min read.

The rule

When weld buildup is applied to the external surface of an internally thinned pressure component, ASME PCC-2 Article 202 recommends that the first weld pass or passes follow the outer perimeter of the buildup area, before the internal region is filled. After the perimeter is complete, the remaining layers are deposited in accordance with the qualified welding procedure specification (WPS).

Why the perimeter goes first

1. A gauge for the welder

The perimeter pass outlines the buildup on the component before any fill is laid. It shows the welder exactly where the buildup starts and ends, which helps control the final profile and the dimensions of the repair. Filling inside a defined outline is easier and more consistent than building outwards and trying to finish the edge at the end.

2. A better heat-affected zone

In carbon and low-alloy steels, each weld pass creates a heat-affected zone (HAZ) in the base metal next to it. A pass that is never reheated leaves an untempered HAZ, which can be hard and less tough. If the perimeter were welded last, the final pass would sit directly on the base metal at the edge of the buildup, and its HAZ would stay untempered.

Welding the perimeter first changes that. The fill passes laid next to it, and the layers above it, reheat its HAZ and temper it. The last passes of the repair then land on weld metal rather than on the base metal surface.

Then follow the qualified WPS

Once the perimeter is in place, the rest of the buildup is deposited in accordance with the qualified WPS: layer sequence, preheat, interpass temperature and heat input as qualified. The perimeter rule does not replace the WPS; it sets the order in which the WPS is applied.

The buildup itself must also meet the other requirements of Article 202, including the design of its thickness and extent and the required examination. These are outside the scope of this note.

Summary

  • Weld the outer perimeter of the buildup area first.
  • The perimeter acts as a gauge for the profile and dimensions.
  • Later passes temper the HAZ of the perimeter pass in carbon and low-alloy steels.
  • Fill the remaining layers to the qualified WPS.

References

  1. ASME PCC-2, Repair of Pressure Equipment and Piping, Article 202: External Weld Buildup to Repair Internal Thinning.
