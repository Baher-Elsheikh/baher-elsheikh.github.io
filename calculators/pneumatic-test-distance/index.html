How to use this calculator

A pneumatic test stores far more energy than a hydrostatic test at the same pressure, because gas is compressible. If the equipment fails, that energy is released as a blast wave and can throw fragments. ASME PCC-2 Appendix 501-III sets the minimum distance between all personnel and the equipment under test for both effects. The calculator works out the stored energy and its TNT equivalent, the blast wave distance, the fragment throw distance and the governing exclusion distance.

Steps

  1. Choose the test gas. Air and nitrogen use k = 1.4.
  2. Enter the total volume that will be under pressure, including piping and connected equipment, and the test pressure (gauge; pick the unit first). For a vessel the total volume is used. For a piping system, choose the piping basis and enter the pipe inside diameter: PCC-2 allows the volume of a pipe length of 8 diameters for any single failure, if smaller than the total.
  3. Keep Rscaled = 20 unless the full distance cannot be achieved and an alternative has been justified per Table 501-III-1-1 and 501-6.2(g).
  4. Say whether fragments could reach personnel. If yes, the fragment distance of Table 501-III-2-1 is also applied.
  5. Read the governing distance in the banner: the larger of the blast wave and fragment distances, in metres and feet.
  6. Mark and barricade the exclusion zone at that distance before pressurising, and print or save the calculation with the test procedure.

Good practice

  • Reduce the stored energy where you can: fill most of the volume with water or solid fillers, or test sections separately; the distance falls with the cube root of the energy.
  • For E up to 8 130 000 J (6,000,000 ft·lb) the blast distance is a flat 30 m (100 ft); above that it grows with (2 × TNT)⅓.
  • Even at the minimum distance, structural damage such as broken glass is possible; Table 501-III-1-1 lists the consequences for lower scaled factors. Check nearby buildings and equipment.
  • Pressurise in steps with holds, and inspect only at reduced pressure as the test procedure requires.
  • Beyond 10 000 kg TNT, Table 501-III-2-1 ends: evaluate fragment distances by public-domain methods (501-III-2(b)).

Not covered

  • The choice between hydrostatic and pneumatic testing, test pressure, brittle fracture (MDMT) review and the test procedure itself, which are covered elsewhere in PCC-2 Article 501.
