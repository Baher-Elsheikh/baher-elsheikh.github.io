How to use this calculator

The calculator checks a welded plug used to take a leaking heat exchanger tube out of service. The plug is treated as a small welded flat head under the higher of the tube side and shell side pressures. The fillet weld is checked for the pressure force with a design margin, and both are checked again at hydrotest pressure. Every equation is shown with your values in it.

Steps

  1. Choose the arrangement. Fig. 1 when the plug goes inside the tube and is welded to the tube end. Fig. 2 when the tube end is removed and the plug sits in the tubesheet hole. The sketch at the top changes to match.
  2. Enter the design conditions of both sides. Pick the pressure unit first; all pressures, including the test pressure, use it.
  3. Enter the tube and tubesheet dimensions. The pressure diameter Dp is the tube ID less 0.5 mm for Fig. 1, and the hole diameter for Fig. 2.
  4. Enter the materials and their allowable stresses from Section II Part D at the plug design temperature (the higher of the two), and the plug yield at test temperature.
  5. Enter the proposed plug: height, solid thickness at its thinnest point, wall thickness at the weld (0 for a solid plug), fillet weld leg and corrosion allowance.
  6. Keep C = 0.33 and E = 1.0 unless you have a reason to change them.
  7. Enter the hydrotest pressure and tick PWHT if the equipment needed it.
  8. Read the result. The banner turns green only when every check passes: thickness, height, weld shear, weld leg and both hydrotest checks. If one fails, the step that fails tells you which dimension to increase.
  9. Print or save as PDF and attach it to the repair procedure.

Good practice

  • Check both sides: a plug in a high-pressure tube side is usually governed by the tube side pressure, but the shell side acts on the other face.
  • Match the plug material to the tubesheet (Fig. 2) or tube (Fig. 1) for weldability and to avoid galvanic or thermal-expansion problems.
  • If the equipment was post-weld heat treated, the plug weld needs the same treatment, or an alternative accepted by the owner and the inspector.
  • Use a qualified welding procedure and welder, and examine the weld (PT or MT) before the test.
  • Record the plugged tube positions; plugging changes the heat transfer area and, with many plugs, the tube-side velocity.

Basis of the checks

  • The 1.5 factor on the weld force and the hydrotest checks are the author's practice, not code requirements.
  • The throat-stress rows (EN 1993-1-8) are for information only and have no pass / fail.
  • Not covered: explosive or mechanical (non-welded) plugs, tubesheet ligament checks and plugging limits for the bundle.
