Conditions of the method

How to use this calculator

Piping loads on a nozzle flange add a bending moment and an axial force to the internal pressure. UG-44(b) lets you check a standard welding neck flange for these loads by converting them into an equivalent pressure and comparing it with the flange rating increased by a moment factor FM. Code Case 2901-1 uses the same equation with its own, size-dependent moment factors, which are often higher for small flanges. Both require the bolting and the assembly bolt load to meet minimum conditions.

Steps

  1. Choose the method. UG-44(b) uses Table UG-44-1 and the vessel MAWP; Code Case 2901-1 uses its Table 1 and the vessel design pressure.
  2. Enter the pressure (pick the unit first), any static pressure acting on the flange, and the flange rating PR at design temperature from the B16.5 or B16.47 rating table.
  3. Select the nozzle axis and enter the six loads Fx, Fy, Fz, Mx, My, Mz from the piping stress analysis or the nozzle load table, in its global axes. The axis is the direction from the vessel towards the piping (for example +X for a shell nozzle pointing east, +Y for a nozzle on the top head). Step 1 shows the sketch and how the loads combine: FE is the force along the axis when it pulls the flange away, and ME is the resultant of the two moments across the axis.
  4. Enter the gasket contact OD and ID; G is calculated to Mandatory Appendix 2. Or enter G directly.
  5. Enter the moment factor FM from your copy of Table UG-44-1 or Code Case 2901-1 Table 1, for the flange standard, size and class. A size and class with no value in the table is outside the scope of the method.
  6. Confirm the three conditions: welding neck flange, bolting at least as strong as SA-193 B8 Cl. 2, and assembly bolt load per PCC-1 Appendix O.
  7. Read the result and the interaction diagram; step 3 also gives the largest moment and force the flange can take.

Good practice

  • For small flanges, Code Case 2901-1 usually allows much higher loads than UG-44(b); when it is used, record the case number on the Manufacturer's Data Report.
  • Consider a lower FM when the loads are mainly sustained and the joint runs hot enough for gasket creep and relaxation to matter (typically above 232 °C).
  • Set the assembly bolt load with the PCC-1 bolt torque calculator, and check non-standard flanges with the Appendix 2 flange calculator.
  • The two bending moments combine as √(M1² + M2²). Torsion does not open the joint and is left out; tick the box to include it as √(M1² + M2² + Mt²) where a company specification asks for it.

Not covered

  • Slip-on, lap joint and non-standard flanges, compressive axial force (taken as 0), shear force, nozzles not lined up with a global axis (resolve the loads to the nozzle axes first), and the nozzle-to-shell junction (WRC 107 / 297 or FEA).
