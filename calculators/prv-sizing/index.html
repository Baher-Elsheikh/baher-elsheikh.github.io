How to use this calculator

The calculator finds the effective discharge area a pressure relief valve needs for a given relieving rate, picks the API 526 orifice letter, and shows the rated capacity of that orifice. Every equation is shown with your values in it, so the result can be checked line by line.

Steps

  1. Choose the service and units. Gas / vapour for gases and vapours relieving in critical flow; Liquid for non-flashing liquids. Then USC or SI.
  2. Enter the required relieving rate. This comes from the governing overpressure scenario (blocked outlet, fire, control valve failure and so on) worked out to API 521. The calculator does not determine the relief load.
  3. Enter the fluid properties at relieving conditions. Gas: M, k, Z and temperature. Liquid: specific gravity and viscosity.
  4. Enter the pressures. Set pressure and allowable overpressure fix the relieving pressure P1. For gas, give back pressure and atmospheric pressure in absolute units; for liquid, back pressure in gauge units.
  5. Choose the valve type and factors. Start with the preliminary Kd shown. Tick the rupture disk box if a disk is installed upstream of the valve. For balanced-bellows valves, enter Kb or Kw from API 520 Part I or the manufacturer.
  6. Read the required area in step 3 and the orifice in step 4. Leave the orifice on Smallest that fits, or pick a letter to check a valve you already have.
  7. Check the result. The banner at the top turns green when the selected orifice area is at least the required area. Step 5 gives the rated capacity of the selected orifice.
  8. Print or save as PDF to keep the calculation with your inputs.

Good practice

  • Size with preliminary Kd, then repeat with the manufacturer's rated Kd and actual area once the valve is selected. Use either effective area with effective Kd, or actual area with rated Kd: do not mix them.
  • Avoid choosing a much larger orifice than needed. An oversized valve, especially in liquid service, can chatter. The calculator flags this.
  • The orifice letter does not fix the inlet and outlet sizes or the pressure rating. Check the API 526 table for the valve material, temperature and pressure class.
  • Check inlet pressure loss (3% of set pressure) and built-up back pressure to API 520 Part II and the manufacturer.
  • For gas, the flow must be critical: the calculator checks Pb/P1 against the critical pressure ratio and stops if it is not.

Not covered

  • Subcritical gas flow, steam (KN, KSH), two-phase and flashing flow.
  • Relief load calculation (API 521) and inlet / outlet piping hydraulics.
