How to use this calculator

The calculator follows the joint component approach of ASME PCC-1 Appendix O. Steps 1 to 4 set the assembly bolt stress: start from the gasket stress you want, then cap it at the bolt maximum, raise it to the bolt minimum and cap it at the flange limit. Steps 5 to 8 then check the result against the gasket seating, operating and maximum stresses and the flange rotation limit. The selected bolt stress gives the tightening torque. Every equation is shown with your values in it; the example is the NPS 3 Class 300 joint worked in Appendix O, converted to metric.

Steps

  1. Choose the bolt size and number. Metric root areas are taken from PCC-1 Table H-1M; inch root areas from the minor diameter (UNC up to 1 in., 8-thread series above).
  2. Select the bolt limits. PCC-1 O-4.1 gives typical ranges: Sbmax 40% to 70% of ambient bolt yield, to avoid damage to the bolts or assembly equipment; Sbmin 140 to 245 MPa (20 to 35 ksi), so that bolting inaccuracies stay a small part of the bolt stress and the load is high enough to prevent self-loosening. The calculator notes values outside these ranges.
  3. Enter the gasket contact dimensions and stresses from the gasket manufacturer: target, minimum seating, minimum operating and maximum, and the relaxation factor φg.
  4. Enter the maximum pressure and, if known, the flange limits Sfmax and the rotations θgmax and θfmax.
  5. Read steps 1 to 4 for the selected bolt stress, and steps 5 to 8 for the checks. The window at the top shows all limits and where Sbsel sits.
  6. Enter the nut factor for the lubricant actually used, and read the target torque and tightening passes.
  7. Print or save as PDF and attach it to the joint assembly procedure.

Good practice

  • The Appendix O values are not hard limits. If a step 5 to 8 check is not met (as for some NPS 8 Class 150 flanges), use judgement on which limit is more critical to joint integrity and adjust Sbsel with the override.
  • If no bolt stress works, the joint needs a change: a different gasket, stronger bolting, or more or larger bolts.
  • For high-temperature flanges whose yield drops in service, consider the flange operating limit too (see the WRC 538 work on this site).
  • The nut factor method has a scatter of about ±30% in bolt load. For a different nut factor K', torque scales by K / K' (Eq. O-3).
  • Follow the tightening pattern and passes in the qualified procedure; the 30% / 70% / 100% passes shown are the PCC-1 legacy pattern.

Not covered

  • Hydraulic tensioning (load transfer factor), elevated-temperature bolt relaxation beyond the factor φg, and derivation of the flange limits themselves.
