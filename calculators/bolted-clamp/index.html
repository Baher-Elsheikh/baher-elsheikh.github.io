How to use this calculator

A bolted clamp seals a leaking pipe without shutting it down: two split halves are bolted around the line and sealant is injected into the space between clamp and pipe. The calculator follows a simplified method for a non-structural clamp (it contains the leak; it does not reinforce the pipe), metal-to-metal without a gasket, with sealant injection and stable operating conditions.

The clamp and its symbols

Steps

  1. Enter the line conditions. Pick the pressure unit first. The clamp is designed for the higher of the line design pressure and the sealant injection pressure.
  2. Measure the line at site (OD and remaining wall) before sizing the clamp, and set the clamp ID with a gap for the sealant.
  3. Estimate the clamp dimensions: sealed length, lug length, bolt line distance, and proposed shell, end plate and lug thicknesses.
  4. Enter the clamp material and its allowable stresses from Section II Part D, using a material that matches the line.
  5. Choose the bolts: size, number, hole diameter, allowable stresses and the factor of safety on bolt load (10% is typical).
  6. Read steps 2 to 6. If a thickness check fails, increase that thickness. If the bolt check fails, increase the bolt size or number, then check the lug again: more bolt holes reduce the effective lug length.
  7. Set the assembly bolt stress and nut factor to get the tightening torque, and review the recommended checks in steps 7 and 8.
  8. Print or save as PDF and attach it to the repair procedure.

Good practice

  • Check the line itself for external pressure under the sealant injection pressure.
  • If the leak comes from a crack, take special care before clamping; see ASME PCC-2 for guidance.
  • Confirm with the sealant data sheet (MSDS) that the sealant suits the fluid and temperature.
  • Check whether the line supports need adjusting for the clamp weight.
  • Keep the assembly bolt stress between about 40% and 70% of yield and not below about 140 MPa. Low-strength bolts (for example B8 Class 1, yield 205 MPa) cannot meet both.

Basis and limits

  • The lug is treated as a beam: Z = L T²/6 gives T = 2.45 √(M / ((L − nD) Sf)), as for a flange ring in Appendix Y.
  • The bolt-up lug check (step 7) is a recommended addition with the Appendix 2 seating bolt load; it is shown separately from the main result.
  • Not covered: structural clamps (use FEA), gasketed clamps, lug-to-shell and end plate welds.
