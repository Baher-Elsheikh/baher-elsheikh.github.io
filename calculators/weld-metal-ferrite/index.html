How to use this calculator

When stainless steel is welded to itself or to carbon steel, the weld metal is a mixture of the filler and the melted base metals. Its structure (austenite, ferrite, martensite) and its ferrite content control hot cracking, toughness and corrosion. The constitution diagrams predict this structure from two numbers: a chromium equivalent (ferrite formers) and a nickel equivalent (austenite formers). Because both equivalents mix linearly, every possible weld lies on the line between the filler and the base metal mix, at a position set by the dilution.

Steps

  1. Select the welding process. The typical dilution is filled in; overwrite it with your own or a measured value.
  2. Set the share of base metal 1 in the melted base metal (50% for a symmetrical joint; 100% for a cladding or a similar-metal joint, entering the same metal twice).
  3. Enter the chemistry of the two base metals and the filler metal, including nitrogen and copper for DeLong and WRC-1992.
  4. Read the diagrams. Switch between WRC-1992, DeLong and Schaeffler with the tabs. Drag the dilution slider to see how the weld point moves; the small dots mark 0, 10, 20 ... 50% dilution.
  5. Check the results in the report: Ferrite Number, solidification mode, martensite risk and the target range. Print or save the page with the WPS.

Good practice

  • Use WRC-1992 for the Ferrite Number: it is the most accurate of the three (about ±4 FN up to 18 FN) and is the diagram of ASME Section III and the AWS filler metal specifications.
  • Use Schaeffler to check for martensite, which WRC-1992 and DeLong do not show. This matters for stainless-to-carbon-steel joints at high dilution.
  • For carbon steel to stainless joints, an over-alloyed filler such as E309L keeps the root pass in the ferrite-containing austenite field despite the carbon steel dilution. Check the highest realistic dilution, not only the typical value.
  • Primary ferrite solidification (FA mode) and a few FN of ferrite give good resistance to hot cracking.

Not covered

  • Duplex balance at high nitrogen, titanium-stabilised compositions, nickel-base fillers outside the diagrams, cooling-rate effects and ferrite measurement for acceptance (AWS A4.2, ISO 8249).
