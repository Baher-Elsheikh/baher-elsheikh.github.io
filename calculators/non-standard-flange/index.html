How to use this calculator

The calculator designs a bolted flange that is not covered by the standard flange tables, following ASME VIII-1 Mandatory Appendix 2. It finds the bolt loads for gasket seating and operation, the bolt area needed, the flange moments, and the longitudinal hub, radial and tangential ring stresses, and checks them and the rigidity index against the code limits. The flange factors F, V, f, FL and VL are calculated from the equations of Table 2-7.1, so no chart reading is needed. For integral flanges, an optional WRC 538 check tests the flange at the actual assembly bolt stress.

Steps

  1. Choose the flange type per Fig. 2-4: integral (welding neck or welded hub), loose with hub, or loose ring without hub. The half section at the top shows your geometry to scale.
  2. Enter the design pressure (pick the unit first) and the dimensions A, B, C, t and, for hubbed flanges, g0, g1 and h, all in the corroded condition.
  3. Enter the gasket contact OD and ID, and m and y from Table 2-5.1 or the manufacturer. bo = N/2 is used unless you enter bo for another facing sketch.
  4. Choose the bolts and enter the bolt allowables at ambient and design temperature.
  5. Enter the flange allowables Sfo (design) and Sfa (ambient), the hub / nozzle allowable Sn and the flange modulus.
  6. Read the result. The banner turns green when the bolt area, all stress checks for both gasket seating and operation, and the rigidity index pass. Increase t (and g1, h for hubbed flanges) or the bolting if a check fails.
  7. Optional WRC 538: enter the bolt yield, the assembly bolt stress as % of yield, and the flange yield at ambient and design temperature to see whether the flange is overstressed at bolt-up.

Good practice

  • Use corroded dimensions for B, g0, g1 and t; the calculator does not subtract the corrosion allowance.
  • Stress margins of a few percent are common in flange design; the rigidity check is often the one that governs leak tightness.
  • Flanges that pass Appendix 2 can still be overstressed at bolt-up if the bolts are tightened to a high stress; the WRC 538 check shows this. Use the PCC-1 calculator to choose the assembly bolt stress.
  • Check the bolt spacing and the radial clearance to the hub and the edge against TEMA or the bolting supplier when space is tight.

Not covered

  • Reverse flanges, split loose flanges, flat-face flanges with full-face gaskets (Appendix Y), external loads on the joint and flange stresses at elevated temperature beyond the WRC 538 operating check.
