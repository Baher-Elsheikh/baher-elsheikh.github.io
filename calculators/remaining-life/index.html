How to use this calculator

The calculator turns thickness readings at a condition monitoring location (CML) into a corrosion rate, a remaining life and the date of the next inspection, the way API 510 does for pressure vessels and API 570 for piping. It also checks the MAWP the equipment will have at the next inspection, after twice the corrosion expected until then.

Steps

  1. Choose the equipment type. Vessels follow API 510; piping follows API 570 by piping class.
  2. Enter three readings for one CML: the initial thickness and date (installation or first reading), the previous inspection, and the current inspection. Use the minimum reading at the CML.
  3. Choose how to get the required thickness: calculated for a cylindrical shell (UG-27) or straight pipe (B31.3), or entered from your own calculation. Add a structural minimum if one applies.
  4. Read the corrosion rates. The greater of the long-term and short-term rate is used; change the readings if the inspector judges another rate more representative.
  5. Read the remaining life, next inspection and retirement dates, and the MAWP at the next inspection.
  6. Repeat for each CML: the CML with the shortest remaining life governs the equipment.

Good practice

  • A short-term rate much higher than the long-term rate usually means a process change: investigate before relying on the half-life interval.
  • Readings thicker than the previous one (negative rate) usually come from measurement scatter; use the same location, instrument and method each time.
  • Where the current thickness is already below the required thickness, assess fitness for service (API 579-1) or repair before returning to service.
  • Risk-based inspection (API 580 / 581) may justify other intervals; this calculator follows the traditional thickness-based rules.
  • Localized corrosion and pitting need a separate evaluation; this method suits general metal loss.

Not covered

  • Statistical treatment of many CMLs, local thin areas and pitting, heads and nozzles (enter their required thickness directly), and injection-point or soil-to-air intervals.
